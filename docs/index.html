A ticker is coloured by TREND, so a red ticker inside a green “at the low end” chip is a name at the bottom of its range that is below TREND — the one case the handbook says not to buy. VIX and MOVE are the exception: they carry no position, so they are coloured by what they mean for everything else — green when volatility is falling, red when it is rising. The track is the range drawn as a rule: the low edge on the left, the high on the right, tinted green where the model buys and red where it shorts. The bright pin is spot. The two small stems below it are TRADE and TREND, coloured by which side price is on — a stem pinned to either end and dimmed means that line sits outside the range entirely, which TREND often does. The bands themselves move with the VIX rather than being a fixed fraction. Volume is shown as a z-score of log volume against the fund's own 1-month and 3-month distributions; amber marks an unusually heavy session (z ≥ +2) and blue an unusually light one (z ≤ −2).

Position sizing scales with the VIX. Each position shows its size beside the side, as a percent of capital. The unit is 3% when the VIX is calm (<19), 2% in chop (19–29), 1% in stress (≥29) — so a buy in a panic commits less. Per-name caps scale with it: calm equities reach 15%, commodities 12%, fixed income 15%, FX 18%; chop and stress are a half and a third of that; shorts stay small. A position scales in one unit as the signal confirms and out one unit on a trim, closing only at the floor. The book runs cash when few names qualify and sizes DOWN into high vol by design.
